## What a buyer is actually underwriting

Retail is the hardest category on this site to sell well, and being clear about why is more useful than optimism.

A buyer is acquiring earnings that sit on top of a lease. The landlord holds a genuine claim on the value of the location, and exercises it at every renewal. Meanwhile, margin faces continuous pressure from online alternatives that carry no rent at all. Neither of those facts is a comment on how well you run the shop. They are why the starting multiple sits where it does.

What separates retailers who sell well from those who do not is whether there is something the landlord cannot take: a brand people seek out, a customer list you own, or a channel that does not need the premises.

## How the multiple range is applied

The indicative range is 2.5x to 4x normalised EBITDA, applied to profit adjusted for owner salary at a market rate, one-off costs and identifiable personal expenses.

Within it:

- **Lease tenure and assignability.** The single largest factor. Long remaining term with assignment permitted, versus a renewal due next year.
- **Brand pull.** Customers who come for you specifically, evidenced by repeat purchase, rather than passing footfall that belongs to the mall.
- **Channel mix.** A genuine online channel with its own margin and customer data.
- **Stock discipline.** Turns and ageing, which show whether you buy well.

## An illustrative example

Illustrative figures, chosen to show the mechanics.

A three-store specialty retailer turns over S$3.4m with reported profit of S$290,000. The owner takes S$70,000 against the S$130,000 a retail manager would cost, so normalisation reduces earnings by S$60,000, giving normalised EBITDA of about S$230,000.

At 2.5x that is roughly S$575,000, at 4x roughly S$920,000.

Now add the balance sheet. Stock at cost is S$400,000, of which S$90,000 is more than a year old and gets written down. A buyer expects normal working capital in the business at completion. Both of those come out of the seller's side. This is why retail owners are so often surprised by the gap between the enterprise value discussed and the money that arrives, and why clearing dead stock early is one of the highest-return preparations available.

## Making the lease work for you rather than against you

If your remaining term is short, this is the first thing to fix and it usually outranks everything else on this page.

Renew before you market, on terms you can live with, and make sure assignment to a purchaser is permitted without unreasonable conditions. Where the landlord requires consent, understand what they will want, because a consent right is effectively a third party who can hold up your deal at the final stage without being party to it.

## Where to start

Check your remaining lease term today. If it is under two years, that is the project. Then get an indicative range on normalised earnings and take an honest view of your stock ageing. The [exit-readiness check](/exit-readiness) scores the ten factors a buyer verifies.
