## The method does not change with the sector

The twenty categories on this site cover most of what we are asked to sell, but plenty of good businesses do not fit any of them neatly. That is not an obstacle. Sector labels are a shorthand for risk, not a valuation method in themselves.

Every SME valuation runs the same way. Start from normalised earnings. Apply a multiple that reflects how reliable and how transferable those earnings are. Then adjust at completion for cash, debt, working capital and any deferred obligations.

## The four questions that set the multiple

Whatever your industry, a buyer is answering these four, and the answers place you on the scale far more precisely than your sector name does.

**How contracted is the revenue?** Committed, recurring income at one end; work that must be won again each year at the other. This is the largest single factor in every sector on this site.

**How dependent is it on you?** If the customers, the pricing knowledge and the supplier relationships are personal to the owner, then a buyer is acquiring a handover risk rather than a going concern.

**How concentrated are the customers?** One client at forty percent of revenue means the buyer is really underwriting that one relationship.

**What protects the position?** Licences, certifications, scarce premises, owned IP, approved supplier status. Anything a competitor cannot obtain by simply deciding to.

A business scoring well on all four sits comfortably above 5x normalised EBITDA regardless of what it does. One scoring poorly sits near 2.5x even in a sector with attractive headline multiples.

## An illustrative example

Illustrative figures, chosen to show the framework rather than any particular industry.

A specialist services business turns over S$2.2m with reported profit of S$380,000. The owner takes S$90,000 against a S$150,000 market rate for the role, so normalisation reduces earnings by S$60,000. Normalised EBITDA is about S$320,000.

Now apply the four questions. Sixty percent of revenue is on annual renewable contracts with a 90 percent renewal history. A manager runs delivery. The largest client is 12 percent. The business holds a licence that takes two years to obtain. On those answers a buyer is somewhere around 4.5x to 5x, giving roughly S$1.44m to S$1.6m, and the sector label never entered the calculation.

## Where to start

Use the estimator with whichever category is closest, and read the output as a starting point rather than an answer. Then work through the four questions above honestly. If the picture is unclear, the [exit-readiness check](/exit-readiness) scores the ten specific things a buyer verifies, which is usually more useful than a number when you are early in the process.
