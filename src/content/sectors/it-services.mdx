## What a buyer is actually underwriting

In IT services the buyer is asking one question before any other: does this revenue arrive again next year without anyone selling it.

That single distinction explains why the indicative range here, 5x to 9x normalised EBITDA, is the widest on this site. A systems integrator winning projects one at a time and a managed services provider with three-year contracts can report the same profit and be worth twice as different a number. The profit is the same. The confidence is not.

## How the multiple range is applied

Normalised EBITDA is reported profit with owner-specific items adjusted: your salary brought to market for the job you actually do, one-off costs removed, personal expenses stripped where they are identifiable.

Then the buyer sorts your revenue into buckets and weighs them very differently:

- **Contracted recurring.** Managed services agreements, support contracts, subscriptions with a stated term. This is what earns the top of the range.
- **Repeat but uncontracted.** Clients who come back reliably but have no obligation to. Valuable, discounted against contracted.
- **Project and time-and-materials.** Has to be won again. Real revenue, lowest weighting.

Two more factors then move you within the range. Delivery independence, meaning whether projects run without the founder in them. And technical debt, meaning whether a buyer inherits a maintainable stack or a rewrite.

## An illustrative example

Illustrative figures, chosen to show the mechanics.

A managed services provider turns over S$3.6m with reported profit of S$540,000. The founder takes S$100,000 against a market rate of S$180,000 for the delivery director role they occupy, so normalisation reduces earnings by S$80,000. Normalised EBITDA is around S$460,000.

At 5x that is roughly S$2.3m, at 9x roughly S$4.1m. Suppose 70 percent of revenue is on multi-year managed contracts with 90 percent renewal, delivery is run by a technical lead who is staying, and no client exceeds 15 percent. That business is being valued at the upper end. Change one variable, make the founder the lead architect on every major project, and the same S$460,000 is worth considerably less, because a large part of what is being bought leaves on completion day.

## The IP and contractor question

Technical diligence in this sector is more searching than commercial owners expect, and one finding comes up repeatedly: contractors who wrote code without signing an IP assignment.

It matters because the company may not own what it is selling. It is difficult to fix afterwards, particularly where the contractor has moved on or the work was years ago. Auditing your IP chain, including open source licence obligations in anything you ship, is unglamorous work that prevents a late and expensive problem.

## Where to start

Split your revenue into the three buckets above before doing anything else. The proportions will tell you more about your likely valuation than any headline profit figure. Then get an indicative range, and if the gap to your target is wide, it is almost always the mix of contracted to project revenue, which responds to deliberate effort over a year or two.
