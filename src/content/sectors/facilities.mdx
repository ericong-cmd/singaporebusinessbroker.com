## What a buyer is actually underwriting

Facilities services is among the most actively consolidated categories in Singapore, and that changes the selling experience. In many sectors an owner is looking for one buyer. Here there are trade groups and financial buyers actively building scale, which means a well-prepared business can attract genuine competition.

What they are underwriting is the contract book and the ability to staff it. Everything else is secondary.

## How the multiple range is applied

The indicative range is 3x to 5x normalised EBITDA, applied to profit adjusted for owner salary at market, one-off items and identifiable personal expenses.

Within it:

- **Contract length and renewal rate.** Multi-year contracts with a demonstrated renewal history are the core asset.
- **Client mix.** Institutional and government-linked clients bring stability; concentration in one of them brings risk.
- **Licensing.** Current and transferable, with change-of-control implications known.
- **Manpower position.** Quotas, levies and dependency ratios that allow the book to be serviced and grown.

## An illustrative example

Illustrative figures, chosen to show the mechanics.

A cleaning contractor turns over S$5.2m with reported profit of S$480,000. The owner takes S$140,000, roughly market, so no material adjustment there. A one-off equipment write-off of S$50,000 is added back. Normalised EBITDA is about S$530,000.

At 3x that is roughly S$1.59m, at 5x roughly S$2.65m. What decides where in that S$1m spread the business lands is almost entirely the contract schedule: a book of three-year contracts with 85 percent renewal and no client above 20 percent sits at the top, while a book of annual contracts up for tender in six months sits at the bottom, because the buyer may be purchasing a re-tender rather than a revenue stream.

## Prepare the contract schedule first

Of everything on this page, one piece of preparation dominates: build a clean contract schedule and keep it current.

For each contract, show the client, the value, the start and end dates, the renewal terms, the renewal history and the margin. A buyer will build this document whether or not you give it to them, and the version they build from incomplete information will be more pessimistic than yours.

Owners who hand over a credible schedule are treated as running a professional operation, which affects far more than the contract discussion. It sets the tone for the whole diligence.

## Where to start

Build the contract schedule, then get an indicative range on normalised earnings. If the book is short-dated, the highest-value work available to you is converting annual contracts into multi-year ones before you go to market.
