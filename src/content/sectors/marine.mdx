## What a buyer is actually underwriting

In marine and offshore, the physical position often matters more than the profit and loss. Yard space, berth access and certifications are scarce in Singapore and effectively impossible to create from nothing. A buyer acquiring those is acquiring something that cannot be replicated by spending money faster.

Against that, the sector is genuinely cyclical, and buyers have long memories about it. Earnings get examined across years, not quarters.

## How the multiple range is applied

The indicative range is 3x to 5x normalised EBITDA, applied to profit adjusted for owner salary at market, one-off items and identifiable personal expenses, and then smoothed across the cycle.

Within the range:

- **Facility tenure.** Yard, berth or workshop arrangements with real remaining term and assignability.
- **Certifications.** Class approvals and client-specific qualifications, current and transferable.
- **Customer diversification,** particularly across segments rather than only across names.
- **Through-cycle margin,** meaning whether the business stayed profitable in the weak years.

## An illustrative example

Illustrative figures, chosen to show the mechanics.

A ship repair business reports profit of S$1.4m in a strong year, S$300,000 two years earlier and S$650,000 in between. An owner anchoring on S$1.4m and a buyer anchoring on S$300,000 will not reach agreement. A through-cycle figure of around S$780,000, adjusted for a S$100,000 owner salary shortfall against market, gives normalised EBITDA of about S$680,000.

At 3x that is roughly S$2.04m, at 5x roughly S$3.4m.

The most useful thing in that example is not the arithmetic but the framing. The owner who arrives with the smoothed number, and an explanation of why the weak year was weak, controls the conversation. The owner who arrives with the peak year spends it defending a figure that was never going to be paid.

## Facility arrangements deserve early attention

If your yard or berth arrangement is due for renewal, or its transferability is unclear, deal with that before anything else on this page.

A buyer will not pay for capacity they might not have in two years. Conversely, secured long-term facility access is one of the few things in Singapore SME M&A that can pull a valuation above its sector range, because a strategic buyer may be acquiring the business substantially to get it.

## Cyclicality cuts both ways

One point owners in this sector often miss: the cycle affects when you sell as much as what you sell for.

Going to market in a trough means defending earnings a buyer has already discounted. Going to market at an obvious peak invites scepticism about whether it repeats. The strongest position is a year or two past the bottom, when recovery is visible in the numbers but the peak has not yet arrived, because the trend supports your case rather than working against it. That is rarely the moment an owner feels ready, which is precisely why it is worth planning for rather than reacting to.
