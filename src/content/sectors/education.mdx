## What a buyer is actually underwriting

An education business is a promise to deliver teaching that parents already trust. A buyer is asking one question above all others: does that trust attach to the brand and the programme, or to the founder standing at the front of the room.

Where it attaches to the programme, this is one of the better SME sectors to sell in Singapore. Revenue is recurring in substance, parents renew across terms, and the category is consolidating, which means there are real acquirers rather than one occasional buyer. Where it attaches to the founder, the same profit is worth considerably less.

## How the multiple range is applied

The indicative range is 4x to 6.5x normalised EBITDA, which is high for a service business and reflects the recurring enrolment base. Normalised means your reported profit with owner-specific items adjusted: your own salary brought to what it would cost to employ a principal to do your job, one-off costs removed, personal expenses stripped where they can be identified.

Within the range, the factors are:

- **Curriculum documentation.** A written, proprietary curriculum that a competent teacher can deliver is an asset. Teaching that lives in one person's head is not.
- **Retention and renewal.** Enrolment retention across at least three intakes, evidenced rather than asserted.
- **Teacher stability.** Low turnover, and a principal who intends to stay.
- **Number of centres.** One centre is a business with a single point of failure. Three or more with consistent economics is a platform, and platforms attract competitive bidding.
- **Compliance.** MOE registration and premises compliance current at every branch.

## An illustrative example

Illustrative figures, chosen to show the mechanics.

A two-centre enrichment group turns over S$2.4m with reported profit of S$430,000. The founder takes S$90,000 and also teaches roughly half the classes. Replacing them properly means a principal at S$120,000 plus additional teaching hours costing S$70,000, so normalisation reduces earnings by S$100,000. Normalised EBITDA is around S$330,000.

At 4x that is roughly S$1.32m, at 6.5x roughly S$2.15m. But note what the normalisation revealed: the reported profit was partly the founder working two jobs. That is not a criticism of how the business was run. It is simply the adjustment any buyer makes, and it is better to make it yourself first so the number you have in mind is realistic.

Then there is deferred revenue. If S$180,000 of fees have been collected for teaching not yet delivered, that is deducted at completion. The proceeds are the enterprise value adjusted for cash, debt and this liability, which is why the figure an owner has in their head and the figure that lands in their account can differ substantially.

## Making the teaching transferable

If you take one thing from this page, take this: the work that raises an education valuation most is writing down what you know.

A curriculum that exists as documents, lesson plans and teacher training materials can be delivered by someone else, marketed on its own merits, and replicated in a new centre by a buyer with capital. The same teaching, undocumented, is a personal service business. The gap between those two valuations is larger in this sector than in almost any other on this site, and closing it is a matter of effort rather than money.

The second thing is to shift the marketing from the founder to the brand while you still have time for the change to show in the enrolment numbers. A buyer looking at three intakes of stable enrolment under brand-led marketing is looking at evidence. A buyer being told it would be fine is not.

## Where to start

Get an indicative range on your normalised figures, then work out your deferred revenue position so you know what would actually reach you. If the gap to your target is wide, it is usually curriculum documentation and founder dependency, and twelve to eighteen months is enough to move both.
