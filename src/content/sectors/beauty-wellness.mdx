## What a buyer is actually underwriting

Two things decide the value of a beauty or wellness business, and neither of them is revenue.

The first is whether clients belong to the business or to a person. The second is how much money has been collected for treatments that have not yet been given. Owners tend to think about the first and get caught by the second.

## How the multiple range is applied

The indicative range is 2.5x to 4.5x normalised EBITDA, applied to profit adjusted for owner salary at market, one-off costs and identifiable personal expenses.

Within it:

- **Membership and recurring revenue** versus one-off treatment sales.
- **Therapist retention,** and whether clients book by name or by the brand.
- **Deferred package liability,** the smaller relative to profit, the better.
- **Number of outlets** and consistency of economics across them.
- **Lease tenure,** since these are location businesses.

## An illustrative example

Illustrative figures, chosen to show the mechanics, and this one is worth reading closely.

A two-outlet spa turns over S$1.8m with reported profit of S$260,000. The owner takes S$80,000 against S$120,000 for a manager, so normalisation reduces earnings by S$40,000. Normalised EBITDA is about S$220,000.

At 2.5x that is roughly S$550,000, at 4x roughly S$880,000, at the top of the range roughly S$990,000.

Now the deferred liability. The business has sold packages and holds S$620,000 of client credit for treatments not yet delivered. That obligation transfers to the buyer, and it is deducted. On a mid-range valuation of around S$770,000, the owner's actual proceeds are a fraction of the number they had in mind, and could in principle be negative.

This is not a pessimistic example. It is the most common shape of a beauty and wellness sale, and it is why this page raises the point before anything else. An owner who understands it early can manage package sales in the run-up, deliver down the outstanding balance, and go to market with a liability that does not consume the price.

## Managing the package position before you sell

Two practical moves change this materially.

Shift the commercial model towards memberships billed monthly rather than large prepaid packages. Monthly recurring revenue is worth more to a buyer and creates no deferred liability.

Then work the existing balance down deliberately, encouraging clients to use what they have paid for in the year before you market the business. It costs you delivery capacity now and converts directly into proceeds later.

## Where to start

Calculate your outstanding package liability today, as a number. Compare it against your annual normalised profit. That ratio will tell you more about your realistic outcome than any multiple will.
