## What a buyer is actually underwriting

Every e-commerce valuation comes down to one question: do you own your demand, or do you rent it.

An owned audience, meaning an email list, repeat customers and direct traffic, is an asset that transfers with the business and keeps producing. Rented demand, meaning paid traffic and marketplace placement, produces revenue right up until the moment the platform changes its rules or the auction gets more expensive. Buyers know the difference and price it accordingly.

## How the multiple range is applied

The indicative range is 3x to 6x normalised EBITDA, applied to profit adjusted for owner salary at a market rate, one-off costs and identifiable personal expenses.

Within it:

- **Traffic ownership.** Email list size and engagement, direct traffic share, repeat purchase rate.
- **Channel concentration.** Whether one platform or one ad channel dominates.
- **Contribution margin.** After shipping, returns, payment fees and acquisition cost, not gross margin.
- **Supplier position.** Exclusivity, terms and whether the supplier could go direct.
- **Operational independence.** Whether fulfilment and customer service run without the founder.

## An illustrative example

Illustrative figures, chosen to show the mechanics.

An online retailer turns over S$2.6m with reported profit of S$340,000. The owner takes S$60,000 against S$120,000 to hire someone to run it, so normalisation reduces earnings by S$60,000. Normalised EBITDA is about S$280,000.

At 3x that is roughly S$840,000, at 6x roughly S$1.68m.

Two businesses at that same S$280,000 can sit at opposite ends. One has 40 percent of revenue from repeat customers, a 60,000 person email list producing sales without paid spend, and fulfilment handled by a third party. The other gets 80 percent of orders from one marketplace where it ranks well today. The second is worth much less, because the buyer cannot be confident the ranking survives, and there is no customer list to fall back on.

## Build the owned audience before you sell

If your traffic is mostly rented, the single most valuable thing you can do in the year before a sale is convert some of it into an owned audience.

Capture email at purchase, run genuine retention marketing, and measure repeat purchase rate as a headline metric rather than an afterthought. A rising repeat rate over four consecutive quarters is evidence a buyer can act on, and it changes the character of what you are selling.

## Where to start

Work out what share of your last twelve months of revenue came from repeat customers and direct traffic. That percentage is the best single predictor of where in the range you will land.
